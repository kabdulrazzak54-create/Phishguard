Phishing awareness

General educational advice that applies broadly. Rules and reporting channels differ by organisation and region.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a trick where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organisation (via email, SMS, WhatsApp, social media or fake websites) to make you share sensitive information or install harmful software.

What is QR-code phishing (quishing)?

Quishing hides a malicious link inside a QR code, for example on a sticker, poster or message. You cannot read the link by eye, so scan carefully, check the preview your phone shows, and be wary of QR codes that ask for logins or payments.

Common red flags

  • Urgent or threatening language
  • Unexpected prizes, refunds or gifts
  • Look-alike domains or misspellings
  • Requests for OTP, PIN or passwords
  • Shortened or very long links
  • Links using IP addresses or an @ symbol

How to inspect links safely

  • Read the exact domain, the part just before the first single slash
  • On a computer, hover to preview; on a phone, long-press to see the address
  • Do not open unknown links just to "see what happens"
  • Type the official address yourself or use the official app
  • Paste the text (not click) into PhishGuard

Never share these

Genuine organisations do not need your OTP, UPI PIN, password, CVV or bank details from a message or call. Anyone asking for them is a red flag, even if they sound official.

After clicking a suspicious link

  • Close the page and do not enter anything
  • If you entered a password, change it everywhere you reuse it
  • Enable multi-factor authentication
  • If money or cards may be involved, contact your bank right away
  • Run a security scan on your device if you downloaded a file

How to report suspicious messages

Use the "Report phishing/spam" option in your email app or messenger, report the account to the platform, tell the impersonated organisation through its official channels, and inform your IT/college admin if it targets your institution. Then block the sender.

Safety checklist

  1. Pause before clicking
  2. Check the exact domain
  3. Avoid sharing credentials or OTP
  4. Use official apps/websites
  5. Turn on multi-factor authentication
  6. Report and block suspicious accounts
